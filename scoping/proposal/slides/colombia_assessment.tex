\title{Prenatal or Postnatal?}
\subtitle{The long-run damage of early-life violence, from the end of paramilitary violence in Colombia}
\author{Idea 1: statement and assessment}
\date{\muted{October 2026}}
\begin{document}

%% 1
\begin{frame}{Question}
\lead{Is the long-run damage of early-life exposure to violence prenatal or postnatal?}
\begin{itemize}
\item If prenatal: the channel is maternal stress in pregnancy (fetal origins). Policy targets pregnant women.
\item If postnatal: the channel is the child's environment after birth (caregiver, household, direct exposure). Policy targets early childhood.
\item Today only the prenatal effect is credible: León (2012, \emph{JHR}) found both, and its full-census replication (Huaroto, Gonzales and Pacheco, 2026, \emph{JHR}) keeps only the in-utero effect.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%% 2
\begin{frame}{Setting: a violence that ends on a known date}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.55\textwidth}
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_en.pdf}
\column{0.43\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
\item Paramilitary blocs demobilize one by one, November 2003 to August 2006; zero victims after 2007.
\item For each municipality $m$ we observe the month $T_m$ when violence ended.
\item Children born in the same municipality differ in their age at $T_m$: born after it, never exposed; born just before, exposed only in utero; born earlier, exposed also in infancy or preschool.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

%% 3
\begin{frame}{Best specification}
Person $i$ born in municipality $m$ in cohort $c$; $k = c - T_m$ is age at the end of violence.
\begin{equation*}
Y_{imc} = \sum_{k \ne -1} \beta_k\, \mathbf{1}[c - T_m = k] + \mu_m + \lambda_{c \times d(m)} + \varepsilon_{imc}
\end{equation*}
\begin{itemize}
\item Reference: cohorts born the year after the end (never exposed). $\beta_0$: exposed only in utero. $\beta_{1},\beta_{2}$: also in infancy. $\beta_{3}$ to $\beta_{5}$: also in preschool.
\item \textbf{Test:} $\beta_0 \ne 0$ and $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_0$ means prenatal only; $\beta_k$ rising with $k$ means postnatal damage.
\item Estimated with Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) or de Chaisemartin and D'Haultf\oe uille (2024) for staggered timing; pre-conception placebos; Rambachan and Roth (2023) bounds.
\item Location fixed before the shock: municipality of birth. Outcomes at 20--36: schooling, employment, earnings (GEIH), or test scores at 17 (Saber 11).
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%% 4
\begin{frame}{Contribution}
\begin{itemize}
\item The first separation of prenatal from postnatal damage of violence with an exogenous end date, set by a national negotiation rather than by local conditions.
\item A mechanism test: by displacement of the household (measured in the migration module) and by maternal health at birth (vital statistics).
\item A credible answer to a question the 2026 replication left open.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%% 5
\begin{frame}{Assessment}
\lead{Two problems, and they are decisive.}
\textbf{Already well covered.} Age-at-exposure designs with birth location and cohort fixed effects: Bundervoet, Verwimp and Akresh (2009, \emph{JHR}); Akresh, Bhalotra, Leone and Osili (2012, \emph{AER P\&P}); León (2012, \emph{JHR}); Duque (2024, \emph{JCR}); Akbulut-Yuksel, Tekin and Turan (2025, \emph{JHR}). The prenatal-versus-postnatal angle is new; the design is not.
\vspace{0.8em}

\textbf{Too little variation.} 70\% of municipal end dates fall within eighteen months (July 2004 to December 2005). The identifying groups are thin: exposed only in utero means born in a nine-month window; never exposed means born after 2005, adults only from 2026 onwards. With current data, cohorts born after the end exist in 28 municipalities.
\vspace{0.8em}

\textbf{Verdict.} A good question with a weak instrument. Not a job market paper unless a setting with more dispersed end dates, or linked birth records, is found.
\end{frame}
